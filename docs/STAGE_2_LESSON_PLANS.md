# Stage 2 lesson plans: Grammar Foundations (A1–A2)

Content lives in [`curriculum/stage-2.json`](../curriculum/stage-2.json). This document explains the teaching design and summarises every lesson. If you change a lesson, update both.

## Why this stage exists

Stage 1 (the original `urdu_curriculum.json`) is mostly a phrasebook: greetings, shopping, travel and emergencies, learned as fixed phrases. That gets a learner talking quickly, but they can't yet build new sentences of their own. Stage 2 teaches the **patterns underneath** those phrases, so learners can make sentences they've never seen:

| Unit | Grammar engine | Why it comes here |
|---|---|---|
| Me, You & "To Be" | Pronouns, ہونا (to be) and respectful speech | Every sentence needs a subject and ہونا; respect (آپ) is built into the grammar from day one. |
| Boy, Girl & Agreement | Noun gender, plurals, adjectives and colours | Gender affects almost every word that follows: adjectives, possessives and verbs. |
| Mine, Yours & Family | Possessives, کا / کی / کے and family on both sides | Possessives reuse gender agreement; the family words are culturally essential and very distinct in Urdu. |
| Numbers & Counting | 1-20, bigger numbers, prices and days of the week | A short break from heavy grammar; numbers come up constantly (prices, ages, time). |
| Everyday Actions | Verbs, the present tense, negatives and yes/no questions | Verbs bring all the earlier agreement together: میں پیتا / پیتی ہوں. |
| Likes, Needs & Feelings | مجھے ... پسند ہے، چاہیے، feelings and weather | The مجھے ... (experiencer) pattern is the biggest 'Urdu thinks differently' moment; it's saved for when the basics are solid. |

## How every lesson is built

Every lesson is a short, predictable cycle, so learners always know what's coming:

1. **Recognise.** Learners see and hear each new Urdu word or phrase (with its romanized pronunciation) and pick its meaning. Pictures are shown for concrete nouns.
2. **Produce.** Learners see the English and pick the Urdu. Recall is harder than recognition, so it comes second.
3. **Context.** A 3–4 line dialogue uses the new language in a realistic situation; learners pick the meaning of each line.
4. **Check.** 2–3 hand-written questions target the lesson's *grammar point* (for example choosing ہوں / ہو / ہیں), not just vocabulary.

The app builds steps 1–3 from the lesson's `vocab`, `phrases` and `dialogue`. Wrong answers are drawn from the same lesson first, then the same unit, so the choices are always plausible (no "Thing" / "Unknown" fillers). The 4th lesson in each unit is a review that recycles the whole unit in one conversation.

**Design principles**
- 5–7 new items per lesson. That's within the range a beginner can hold in working memory.
- Each grammar point is introduced with a rule of thumb **and** its main exception (for example آدمی is masculine).
- Spiral review: words from earlier units come back in later dialogues (گھر, بلی, چائے, کتاب…).
- Culturally grounded: respectful address (آنٹی, بیٹا), family terms for each side of the family, جمعہ, cricket, mangoes, chai.
- Gendered speech is explicit: where male and female speakers say things differently, both forms are taught.

## Lesson by lesson

### Unit 1: Me, You & "To Be"

_Pronouns, ہونا (to be) and respectful speech_

#### 1.1 I Am, You Are

**Objectives**
- Use میں، تم، آپ as subjects
- Match each pronoun with ہوں / ہو / ہیں
- Notice that the verb comes last (subject-object-verb)

**Teaching note:** Urdu sentences end with the verb. ہونا (to be) changes with the subject: میں ... ہوں، تم ... ہو، آپ ... ہیں. آپ always takes the plural form ہیں, even for one person - that is what makes it respectful.

**New words:** میں (I), تم (you (informal)), آپ (you (respectful)), ہوں (am (with میں)), ہو (are (with تم)), ہیں (are (with آپ / plural))

**Dialogue:** آپ کون ہیں؟ / میں علی ہوں۔ / کیا آپ استاد ہیں؟ / نہیں، میں طالب علم ہوں۔

#### 1.2 He, She, We & They

**Objectives**
- Use یہ (near) and وہ (far) for he, she, it and they
- Use ہم (we) with ہیں
- Use ہے (is) for one person or thing

**Teaching note:** Urdu has no separate words for 'he' and 'she'. یہ means this person/thing nearby and وہ means that person/thing further away. Gender shows up in the verb and adjectives instead, which comes in Unit 2.

**New words:** یہ (this / he / she (near)), وہ (that / he / she (far)), ہم (we), ہے (is), دوست (friend), بہن (sister)

**Dialogue:** یہ کون ہے؟ / یہ میرا دوست ہے۔ / اور وہ؟ / وہ میری بہن ہے۔

#### 1.3 Tum or Aap? Speaking Respectfully

**Objectives**
- Choose آپ for elders, strangers and anyone you respect
- Use جی as a polite 'yes'
- Address older people warmly as آنٹی، انکل، آپا، بھائی جان

**Teaching note:** Respect is built into Urdu grammar. Use آپ with elders and people you don't know; تم is for close friends and children. Calling an older stranger آنٹی or انکل is normal and friendly. Elders often call younger people بیٹا (child) affectionately.

**New words:** جی (yes (polite)), ہاں (yes (casual)), نہیں (no / not), آنٹی (aunty (any older woman)), انکل (uncle (any older man)), بیٹا (child / dear (said by elders))

**Dialogue:** السلام علیکم آنٹی! / وعلیکم السلام بیٹا۔ تم کیسے ہو؟ / جی، میں ٹھیک ہوں۔ آپ کیسی ہیں؟ / میں بھی ٹھیک ہوں۔

#### 1.4 Review: Meeting the Neighbours

**Objectives**
- Introduce yourself and someone with you
- Use یہاں / وہاں (here / there)
- Respond to خوش آمدید (welcome)

**Teaching note:** This lesson recycles every pattern from the unit in one realistic conversation. Encourage learners to read the dialogue aloud in both roles.

**New words:** پڑوسی (neighbour), نیا (new), یہاں (here), وہاں (there), گھر (house / home)

**Dialogue:** السلام علیکم! ہم نئے پڑوسی ہیں۔ / وعلیکم السلام! خوش آمدید۔ / میں سارہ ہوں، اور یہ میرا بھائی ہے۔ / آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی۔

### Unit 2: Boy, Girl & Agreement

_Noun gender, plurals, adjectives and colours_

#### 2.1 Masculine & Feminine

**Objectives**
- Know that every Urdu noun is masculine or feminine
- Use the -ا (masculine) and -ی (feminine) ending pattern
- Remember the common exception آدمی (man, masculine)

**Teaching note:** Rule of thumb: nouns ending in ا are usually masculine (لڑکا) and nouns ending in ی are usually feminine (لڑکی). Exceptions exist - آدمی ends in ی but is masculine - so learn each noun together with its gender.

**New words:** لڑکا (boy), لڑکی (girl), آدمی (man), عورت (woman), کتا (dog), بلی (cat)

**Dialogue:** یہ لڑکا کون ہے؟ / یہ احمد ہے۔ / اور وہ لڑکی؟ / وہ زینب ہے۔

#### 2.2 One and Many (Plurals)

**Objectives**
- Make masculine -ا nouns plural with -ے
- Make feminine -ی nouns plural with -یاں
- Make other feminine nouns plural with -یں

**Teaching note:** Plural patterns: لڑکا → لڑکے، کمرہ → کمرے (masc. -a → -e); لڑکی → لڑکیاں (fem. -i → -iyan); کتاب → کتابیں (fem. consonant → -ein). Masculine nouns ending in a consonant don't change: ایک دوست، دو دوست.

**New words:** لڑکے (boys), لڑکیاں (girls), کتاب (book), کتابیں (books), کمرہ (room), کمرے (rooms)

**Dialogue:** لڑکے کہاں ہیں؟ / لڑکے باہر ہیں۔ / اور لڑکیاں؟ / لڑکیاں کمرے میں ہیں۔

#### 2.3 Describing Things

**Objectives**
- Make -ا adjectives agree: اچھا (m), اچھی (f), اچھے (pl)
- Place the adjective before the noun
- Recognise adjectives that never change, like خوبصورت

**Teaching note:** Adjectives ending in ا change to match the noun: اچھا لڑکا، اچھی لڑکی، اچھے لڑکے. Adjectives ending in anything else stay the same: خوبصورت لڑکا، خوبصورت لڑکی.

**New words:** اچھا (good (masc.)), اچھی (good (fem.)), بڑا (big (masc.)), چھوٹی (small (fem.)), لمبا (tall / long (masc.)), خوبصورت (beautiful)

**Dialogue:** آپ کا گھر کیسا ہے؟ / میرا گھر بڑا اور خوبصورت ہے۔ / اور آپ کی بلی؟ / میری بلی چھوٹی ہے۔

#### 2.4 Colours

**Objectives**
- Name six basic colours
- Apply agreement to colour words (نیلا / نیلی)
- Notice that لال and سفید never change

**Teaching note:** Colours are adjectives, so the -ا ones agree: نیلا آسمان (sky, m) but نیلی گاڑی (car, f). لال and سفید don't end in ا, so they never change.

**New words:** لال (red), نیلا (blue), ہرا (green), پیلا (yellow), کالا (black), سفید (white)

**Dialogue:** آپ کا پسندیدہ رنگ کون سا ہے؟ / میرا پسندیدہ رنگ ہرا ہے۔ / میرا نیلا ہے۔

### Unit 3: Mine, Yours & Family

_Possessives, کا / کی / کے and family on both sides_

#### 3.1 My & Your

**Objectives**
- Use میرا / میری / میرے (my)
- Use آپ کا / آپ کی (your, respectful)
- Make the possessive agree with the thing owned

**Teaching note:** The key idea: 'my' agrees with the thing you own, not with you. A woman says میرا گھر (house is masc.) and a man says میری کتاب (book is fem.).

**New words:** میرا (my (masc. thing)), میری (my (fem. thing)), میرے (my (plural / respected)), آپ کا (your (masc. thing)), آپ کی (your (fem. thing)), نام (name)

**Dialogue:** کیا یہ آپ کی کتاب ہے؟ / جی نہیں، یہ میری کتاب نہیں ہے۔ / پھر کس کی ہے؟ / شاید علی کی ہے۔

#### 3.2 Whose Is It? (کا، کی، کے)

**Objectives**
- Show possession with کا / کی / کے ('s / of)
- Ask 'whose?' with کس کا / کس کی
- Choose کا / کی / کے by the gender of the thing owned

**Teaching note:** کا / کی / کے work like English 's but come after the owner and agree with the thing owned: علی کا قلم (pen, m), علی کی کتاب (book, f), علی کے دوست (friends, pl).

**New words:** کا ('s / of (masc.)), کی ('s / of (fem.)), کے ('s / of (plural)), کس کا (whose), قلم (pen)

**Dialogue:** یہ قلم کس کا ہے؟ / یہ میرے دوست کا ہے۔ / اور یہ کتاب؟ / وہ سارہ کی ہے۔

#### 3.3 Father's Side of the Family

**Objectives**
- Name grandparents and relatives on the father's side
- Tell چچا (younger) and تایا (older) apart
- Use respectful plural ہیں for elders

**Teaching note:** Urdu has a separate word for each relative depending on which side of the family they're on. Father's side: دادا، دادی، چچا (father's younger brother)، تایا (father's elder brother)، پھوپھو (father's sister). Elders take the respectful plural: میرے دادا مہربان ہیں.

**New words:** ابو (dad), امی (mum), دادا (grandfather (father's father)), دادی (grandmother (father's mother)), چچا (uncle (father's younger brother)), تایا (uncle (father's elder brother)), پھوپھو (aunt (father's sister))

**Dialogue:** اس تصویر میں کون ہیں؟ / یہ میرے دادا اور دادی ہیں۔ / اور یہ؟ / یہ میری پھوپھو ہیں۔

#### 3.4 Mother's Side & Siblings

**Objectives**
- Name grandparents and relatives on the mother's side
- Talk about brothers, sisters and cousins
- Ask how many siblings someone has

**Teaching note:** Mother's side: نانا، نانی، ماموں (mother's brother)، خالہ (mother's sister). A useful contrast drill: دادا / نانا، دادی / نانی، پھوپھو / خالہ، چچا / ماموں.

**New words:** نانا (grandfather (mother's father)), نانی (grandmother (mother's mother)), ماموں (uncle (mother's brother)), خالہ (aunt (mother's sister)), بھائی (brother), کزن (cousin)

**Dialogue:** آپ کے کتنے بہن بھائی ہیں؟ / میرا ایک بھائی اور دو بہنیں ہیں۔ / اور کزن؟ / بہت سارے!

### Unit 4: Numbers & Counting

_1-20, bigger numbers, prices and days of the week_

#### 4.1 One to Five

**Objectives**
- Count from 1 to 5
- Say how many of something you have
- Ask کتنے؟ (how many?)

**Teaching note:** Numbers come before the noun and the noun goes plural from two upward: ایک کتاب، دو کتابیں. میرے پاس (lit. 'near me') is how Urdu says 'I have'.

**New words:** ایک (one (1)), دو (two (2)), تین (three (3)), چار (four (4)), پانچ (five (5))

**Dialogue:** آپ کے پاس کتنے قلم ہیں؟ / میرے پاس تین قلم ہیں۔ / اور کتابیں؟ / صرف ایک!

#### 4.2 Six to Ten

**Objectives**
- Count from 6 to 10
- Say your age with سال کا / سال کی
- Ask عمر کتنی ہے؟ (how old?)

**Teaching note:** Age agrees with the person: a boy says میں نو سال کا ہوں and a girl says میں نو سال کی ہوں - the same کا / کی agreement from Unit 3.

**New words:** چھ (six (6)), سات (seven (7)), آٹھ (eight (8)), نو (nine (9)), دس (ten (10))

**Dialogue:** تمہاری عمر کتنی ہے؟ / میں نو سال کا ہوں۔ / اور تمہاری بہن؟ / وہ سات سال کی ہے۔

#### 4.3 Bigger Numbers & Prices

**Objectives**
- Recognise 11, 12, 15, 20, 100 and 1000
- Ask a price with کتنے کا ہے؟
- Understand a price in rupees

**Teaching note:** Numbers 11-99 in Urdu are irregular, so learners memorise them gradually. Start with the ones used most in shops: 12, 15, 20, 50, 100, 1000. Full 11-20 list for practice: گیارہ، بارہ، تیرہ، چودہ، پندرہ، سولہ، سترہ، اٹھارہ، انیس، بیس.

**New words:** گیارہ (eleven (11)), بارہ (twelve (12)), پندرہ (fifteen (15)), بیس (twenty (20)), سو (hundred (100)), ہزار (thousand (1000))

**Dialogue:** یہ کتنے کا ہے؟ / بیس روپے کا۔ / اور وہ؟ / وہ پندرہ روپے کا ہے۔

#### 4.4 Days of the Week

**Objectives**
- Name the seven days
- Ask and say what day it is
- Understand that کل means both 'yesterday' and 'tomorrow'

**Teaching note:** The week is often thought of as starting on پیر (Monday). جمعہ (Friday) is special in Muslim households - many families go to Friday prayers. کل means yesterday or tomorrow; the verb tense tells you which.

**New words:** پیر (Monday), منگل (Tuesday), بدھ (Wednesday), جمعرات (Thursday), جمعہ (Friday), ہفتہ (Saturday / week), اتوار (Sunday)

**Dialogue:** آج کیا دن ہے؟ / آج جمعرات ہے۔ / یعنی کل جمعہ ہے!

### Unit 5: Everyday Actions

_Verbs, the present tense, negatives and yes/no questions_

#### 5.1 Action Words

**Objectives**
- Recognise the dictionary form of verbs (ending in نا)
- Learn seven high-frequency verbs
- Combine a noun with a verb: پانی پینا

**Teaching note:** Every Urdu verb's dictionary form ends in نا. Remove نا to get the stem: کھانا → کھا، پینا → پی. Learners will build every tense from that stem. Note: کھانا also means 'food'.

**New words:** کھانا (to eat / food), پینا (to drink), جانا (to go), آنا (to come), سونا (to sleep), پڑھنا (to read / study), لکھنا (to write)

**Dialogue:** تم کیا کر رہے ہو؟ / میں کتاب پڑھ رہا ہوں۔ / اچھا! کون سی کتاب؟

#### 5.2 I Eat, I Drink

**Objectives**
- Form the present habitual: stem + تا / تی / تے + ہونا
- Choose تا (male speaker) or تی (female speaker)
- Talk about food and drink you have every day

**Teaching note:** Present habitual = what you usually do. Stem + تا (m) / تی (f) / تے (plural or respectful) + ہونا: میں چائے پیتا ہوں (man), میں چائے پیتی ہوں (woman), ہم چائے پیتے ہیں. The speaker's gender matters, so learners should practise the form that matches them.

**New words:** چائے (tea), پانی (water), روٹی (flatbread (roti)), دودھ (milk), روز (every day)

**Dialogue:** آپ صبح کیا پیتے ہیں؟ / میں چائے پیتی ہوں۔ اور آپ؟ / میں دودھ پیتا ہوں۔

#### 5.3 Not! Saying No

**Objectives**
- Make a sentence negative with نہیں before the verb
- Drop ہے / ہوں in negative sentences, as speakers do
- Use how-often words: ہمیشہ، کبھی کبھی، کبھی نہیں

**Teaching note:** Put نہیں right before the verb: میں گوشت نہیں کھاتا. In everyday speech the final ہوں / ہے is usually dropped in negatives. کبھی نہیں (never) wraps around the verb: وہ کبھی دیر نہیں کرتی.

**New words:** ہمیشہ (always), کبھی کبھی (sometimes), کبھی نہیں (never), گوشت (meat), کافی (coffee)

**Dialogue:** کیا تم کافی پیتے ہو؟ / نہیں، میں کافی نہیں پیتا۔ / اور چائے؟ / ہاں، کبھی کبھی۔

#### 5.4 Yes/No Questions & My Day

**Objectives**
- Turn a statement into a question by adding کیا at the start
- Name the parts of the day
- Ask and answer about daily habits

**Teaching note:** To ask a yes/no question, put کیا at the start and raise your voice at the end: آپ اردو بولتے ہیں → کیا آپ اردو بولتے ہیں؟ Here کیا doesn't mean 'what' - it just marks a question.

**New words:** صبح (morning), دوپہر (afternoon), شام (evening), رات (night), کام (work)

**Dialogue:** کیا آپ اردو بولتے ہیں؟ / جی ہاں، تھوڑی تھوڑی۔ / آپ اردو کب پڑھتے ہیں؟ / میں رات کو پڑھتا ہوں۔

### Unit 6: Likes, Needs & Feelings

_مجھے ... پسند ہے، چاہیے، feelings and weather_

#### 6.1 I Like... (پسند)

**Objectives**
- Say what you like with مجھے ... پسند ہے
- Ask what someone likes with آپ کو ... پسند ہے؟
- Say what you don't like

**Teaching note:** Urdu says 'to me, X is liked': مجھے آم پسند ہے. The person who likes takes کو (میں → مجھے، ہم → ہمیں، آپ → آپ کو). This same pattern is used for needs and feelings in the next lessons.

**New words:** پسند (liked / liking), مجھے (to me), آپ کو (to you (respectful)), آم (mango), سیب (apple), کرکٹ (cricket)

**Dialogue:** آپ کو کون سا پھل پسند ہے؟ / مجھے آم بہت پسند ہے! / مجھے بھی!

#### 6.2 I Need... (چاہیے)

**Objectives**
- Ask for things with مجھے ... چاہیے
- Ask someone what they need
- Shop for everyday items politely

**Teaching note:** چاہیے uses the same 'to me' pattern as پسند: مجھے پانی چاہیے (I need water). It covers both 'need' and a polite 'want', which makes it one of the most useful words for travellers.

**New words:** چاہیے (needed / wanted), ہمیں (to us), مدد (help), کرسی (chair), چابی (key)

**Dialogue:** جی، آپ کو کیا چاہیے؟ / مجھے دو روٹیاں اور دودھ چاہیے۔ / اور کچھ؟ / نہیں، شکریہ۔

#### 6.3 How Do You Feel?

**Objectives**
- Describe feelings: happy, sad, tired, hungry, thirsty, ill
- Make feeling adjectives agree (تھکا ہوا / تھکی ہوئی)
- Ask what's wrong: کیا ہوا؟

**Teaching note:** Feelings use ہونا with an adjective: میں خوش ہوں. Adjectives ending in ا agree with the speaker: a man is بھوکا، a woman is بھوکی. تھکا ہوا / تھکی ہوئی (tired) has two parts and both agree.

**New words:** خوش (happy), اداس (sad), تھکا ہوا (tired (masc.)), بھوکا (hungry (masc.)), پیاسا (thirsty (masc.)), بیمار (ill / sick)

**Dialogue:** کیا ہوا؟ تم اداس کیوں ہو؟ / میں بیمار ہوں۔ / اوہ! آرام کرو۔

#### 6.4 Weather & Review

**Objectives**
- Talk about the weather with گرمی، سردی، بارش
- Say you feel hot or cold with مجھے ... لگ رہی ہے
- Combine likes, needs and feelings in one conversation

**Teaching note:** Weather words are nouns: آج گرمی ہے (lit. 'today there is heat'). Feeling hot or cold uses the 'to me' pattern again: مجھے سردی لگ رہی ہے. The dialogue recycles پسند، چاہیے and feelings from the whole unit.

**New words:** موسم (weather), گرمی (heat / hot weather), سردی (cold / cold weather), بارش (rain), آج (today)

**Dialogue:** آج موسم کیسا ہے؟ / بارش ہو رہی ہے، اور سردی بھی ہے۔ / مجھے گرم چائے چاہیے! / مجھے بھی! مجھے بارش بہت پسند ہے۔

## Ideas for Stage 3 (A2)

Next areas to build, in a sensible order:
1. **Postpositions & places**: میں، پر، سے، تک، کے پاس، کے ساتھ; describing where things are in a room or town.
2. **Past tense**: گیا / گئی، کھایا; the ergative نے ("میں نے کھانا کھایا"). It's a big step, so give it a whole unit.
3. **Future tense**: جاؤں گا / جاؤں گی; making plans.
4. **Imperatives & politeness levels**: کرو / کریں / کیجیے.
5. **Reading Nastaliq**: short real-world texts such as signs, menus and SMS messages.
6. **Compound verbs & 'can'**: سکنا، چکنا.

To add a stage: copy `curriculum/stage-2.json` to `curriculum/stage-3.json`, change the `stage` block, write the units, then run `npm run db:prod` and `npm run assets:report`.
